Structured-first retrieval underperforms on ambiguous queries, study finds

A study of query logs from three enterprise search deployments found that strictly structured retrieval performed worse than semantic search on queries that did not map cleanly onto the content model's vocabulary.

The effect was concentrated in exploratory queries — users who did not yet know the term for what they were looking for. In those cases a controlled vocabulary acted as a filter that excluded relevant material, and the study measured a 22% drop in successful task completion against a semantic baseline.

The authors frame this as a mismatch rather than a defect. "A taxonomy encodes how the organization thinks about its content," the paper argues. "When the user shares that model, it is an accelerator. When the user does not, it is a wall." They observe the same system performed best in the study on queries issued by internal specialists and worst on queries from new employees.

The recommended mitigation is not to abandon structure but to stop treating it as the primary index — using semantic retrieval for recall and structured metadata for filtering and ranking afterwards.

The study's sample is small and drawn entirely from one industry, and the authors decline to generalize beyond it.
