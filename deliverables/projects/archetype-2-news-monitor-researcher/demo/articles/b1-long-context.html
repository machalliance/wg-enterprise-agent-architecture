Long-context models narrow the gap on unstructured corpora

New evaluation results suggest that the accuracy advantage of structured retrieval shrinks substantially as model context windows grow. On a 400,000-token benchmark, feeding raw unstructured documents directly to a long-context model matched the accuracy of a tuned structured-retrieval pipeline on 7 of 9 task categories.

The two categories where structured retrieval still won were multi-hop questions requiring joins across documents, and questions whose answers depended on a field's type rather than its text — distinguishing an effective date from a publication date, for instance.

The cost picture inverts the accuracy picture. The long-context approach consumed roughly 30 times the tokens per query. The authors note that at current prices this makes the structured pipeline cheaper by a wide margin for any high-volume deployment, and that the accuracy parity is therefore "an architectural observation, not yet a procurement recommendation."

One of the evaluation's designers argued the result should be read as a caution against over-investing in content modeling as a permanent moat. "If your entire case for structure is accuracy, that case is weakening every six months," she said. "If your case is cost, provenance, or governance, it is not."

Critics of the benchmark point out that its unstructured corpus was clean prose with few tables, and that document types where structure carries most of the meaning — forms, specifications, regulatory filings — were underrepresented.
